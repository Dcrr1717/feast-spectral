\title{The Stark problem in a Debye plasma: a coupled-channel
complex-scaling map of field--screening competition}

\author{C.~D.~Reinoso}
\email{cesardanielrrch@gmail.com}
\affiliation{Facultad de Ciencias, Escuela Superior Polit\'ecnica de
Chimborazo (ESPOCH), Riobamba, Ecuador}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
A hydrogen atom in a Debye plasma subjected to a static electric field
supports resonances shaped by two competing decay routes: ionization
by the field and dissolution by screening. We compute them with a
coupled-channel complex-scaling method whose radial channels carry the
exact orthonormal Laguerre basis of the unperturbed problem, so the
non-separable system stays below $10^{3}$ degrees of freedom while
reproducing the classical pure-Stark benchmark to ten digits and the
published combined-problem values at interior parameter points to
nine decimals, with every assembly verified at run time against
exact limits. On this
basis we map the ground-state resonance on an 85-point grid of the
$(F,\lambda)$ plane --- to our knowledge the most extensive and
finest-grained such map reported to date --- and follow the $n=2$,
$m=0$ Stark doublet in the screening parameter. Screening amplifies
field ionization exponentially: at $F=0.02$~a.u.\ the width grows by
more than eight orders of magnitude across the screening range,
against a single order at $F=0.10$, quantifying a trend first observed
at isolated parameter points by Yu and Ho. The operational frontier
$\Gamma=|E_r|$ recedes to weaker screening as the field grows, the
perturbative Stark regime shrinks with screening until the width, not
the shift, dominates the field response, and the uphill Stark
component is pushed across $E=0$ --- the ionization threshold of the
field-free problem --- continuing smoothly as a positive-energy
resonance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Stark effect in hydrogen and the screened Coulomb (Yukawa--Debye)
potential are two classical single-parameter problems whose resonance
structure is known to high precision: the pure Stark resonances from
asymptotic and complex-dilation
theory~\cite{DamburgKolosov1976,Herbst1979,Kolosov1987,Telnov1989,
Jentschura2001}, and the screened-Coulomb bound states and critical
screenings from decades of plasma-physics
work~\cite{LamVarshni1971,RogersGraboskeHarwood1970,delValleNader2018,
JanevZhangWang2016}. Their combination --- an atom in a plasma
subjected to an electric field --- is qualitatively richer: the field
tilts the potential, screening lifts and delocalizes the bound states,
and the two decay mechanisms compete. The combined problem is
non-separable (screening breaks the parabolic separability of the
Stark problem), and its record is substantial but scattered: Yu and
Ho computed ground-state positions and widths by complex-coordinate
rotation over a range of Debye lengths and fields~\cite{YuHo2005};
Paul and Ho treated the bound levels, including the $n=2$ splitting,
at the level of energies~\cite{PaulHo2010}; Lin and Ho extended the
complex-scaled treatment to ground and selected excited states with a
Lagrange mesh~\cite{LinHo2011}; bound-state studies at weak fields
continue for generalized screening models~\cite{BaharSoylu2014,
DasGhoshal2025}; and for the exponential-cosine screened potential of
quantum plasmas, Stark resonance energies and widths have been
obtained by complex scaling~\cite{WangChenGuo2019}. What these works
tabulate are selected parameter combinations. What is still missing
is a \emph{map}: dense, uniform two-parameter coverage of positions
and widths under a single error protocol, from which the global
structure of the competition --- amplification laws, critical curves,
the fate of excited manifolds --- can be read directly. To our
knowledge, the present 85-point grid with uniform uncertainty control
is the most extensive such coverage reported for the Debye--H\"uckel
potential.

Two methodological choices make the map cheap enough to be routine.
First, instead of a two-dimensional mesh we exploit the dipole
selection rule: the field couples only adjacent angular channels, so
the problem is a block-tridiagonal radial system in which each channel
carries the \emph{exact} orthonormal Laguerre basis of the
unperturbed radial problem~\cite{companionNR}, and the whole matrix is
complex-scaled analytically. Convergence in the channel cutoff is
geometric, and the classical pure-Stark benchmark is reproduced to ten
digits with fewer than $10^{3}$ degrees of freedom. Second, every
assembly is verified at run time against exact identities. This
discipline earns its keep: during development it exposed, within
seconds, a factor-of-two kinetic-energy defect that had produced a
convergent, rotation-stable spurious state at a plausible energy ---
precisely the failure mode to which complex-rotation width
calculations are known to be sensitive.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

In the $m=0$ sector the Hamiltonian of a hydrogen-like atom in a
Debye plasma and a static field $F\hat z$ (atomic units),
\begin{equation}
H=-\tfrac12\nabla^{2}-\frac{Z e^{-\lambda r}}{r}+F z,
\end{equation}
acts on channel functions $u_{\ell}(r)$ coupled only by the dipole
matrix elements of $z=r\cos\theta$:
$\langle\ell+1,0|\cos\theta|\ell,0\rangle=
(\ell+1)/\sqrt{(2\ell+1)(2\ell+3)}$. Each channel is expanded in the
orthonormal generalized-Laguerre radial functions of
Ref.~\cite{companionNR} with a \emph{common} scale $\beta$ and a
shared quadrature grid across channels, so that the inter-channel
dipole blocks are exact contractions. Production settings: $\beta=
0.35$, $n_b=70$ functions per channel, $\ell_{\max}=14$ (1050 degrees
of freedom), and a 3000-point Gauss--Legendre grid on $x\in[0,760]$,
$x=2\beta r$; the anchors of Table~\ref{tab:anchors} use
$(\ell_{\max},n_b)=(12,60)$ and $(16,80)$. Because the basis is
orthonormal (the overlap identity is certified to $1.3\times10^{-12}$
on every constructed basis), the discretization yields a standard ---
not generalized --- eigenproblem for a complex-symmetric
block-tridiagonal matrix.

Complex scaling $r\to re^{i\theta_c}$ acts analytically on every
block: kinetic terms scale by $e^{-2i\theta_c}$, the Coulomb factor by
$e^{-i\theta_c}$ with the screening exponential evaluated at complex
argument, and the linear Stark coupling by
$e^{+i\theta_c}$~\cite{AguilarCombes1971,BalslevCombes1971,Ho1983,
Reinhardt1982,Moiseyev1998}. For the unbounded Stark term this is
justified not by dilation analyticity of Balslev--Combes type but by
the Herbst--Simon theory of the scaled Stark operator, whose essential
spectrum is empty and whose resonance eigenvalues are locally analytic
in the parameters~\cite{Herbst1979,HerbstSimon1978}. The oscillatory
screened blocks $e^{-\lambda r\cos\theta_c}e^{-i\lambda r
\sin\theta_c}$ are the quadrature stress point; at the largest
$\lambda\theta_c$ used ($\lambda=0.8$, $\theta_c=0.28$) doubling the
grid changes no screened-potential or dipole matrix element by more
than $10^{-10}$.

Resonances are identified by parametric continuation plus rotation
stability. The search window for each new $\lambda$ is predicted as
the exact $F=0$ Yukawa ground level (one symmetric eigensolve) plus
the previous point's complex field shift; on the computed grids the
predictor lands within $\sim\!10^{-3}$ of the eigenvalue while the
nearest competing (rotated-continuum) eigenvalue is an order of
magnitude farther, and the value is accepted only if the rotations
$\theta_c=0.18$ and $0.28$ agree (retry at $0.30/0.42$), the modulus
of their difference being recorded as the spread. Two-angle agreement
alone is a necessary condition, so for the two least favorable points
we computed full $\theta_c$-trajectories over $[0.15,0.45]$: the
broad ground state at $F=0.02$, $\lambda=0.8$ is stationary to
$2\times10^{-9}$ across the entire range, and the positive-energy
$n=2$ component at $\lambda=0.30$ has a true plateau for
$\theta_c\geq0.25$ with residual variation $4\times10^{-5}$ at the
production angles --- consistent with the spreads reported below.

Verification: the hydrogen limit of the assembled system is checked
against the exact levels $-1/2n^{2}$ (deviation $<10^{-10}$ on the
lowest levels) before every production run --- a failed check now
aborts the run --- and the two one-parameter limits are anchored
independently in Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. Quoted uncertainties are
spreads and inter-grid drifts, i.e.\ error \emph{estimates} calibrated
against the benchmarks, not rigorous bounds. A remark on solver
scale: at these dimensions dense complex diagonalization computes
each map point in seconds, so contour-integral eigensolvers, useful
at mesh scales, are unnecessary here.

\subsection{Validation anchors}

Table~\ref{tab:anchors} collects the pure-Stark ground-state anchors.
Each entry is computed on two independent discretizations and two
rotation angles; the fine-grid value is quoted and the inter-grid
drift estimates the truncation error. At $F=0.10$ the result agrees
with the classical complex-dilation value~\cite{Kolosov1987,
Telnov1989,Jentschura2001} on all quoted digits. In the opposite

gives $E=0.003064450-0.002184741i$, within $2.4\times10^{-7}$ of the
triple-verified benchmark of the one-dimensional
engine~\cite{companionNR} --- the two edges of the parameter plane
are anchored independently.

The interior of the plane is validated directly against the published
combined-problem literature (Table~\ref{tab:lit}). At every point
where our grid overlaps the tabulations of Yu and Ho~\cite{YuHo2005}

different discretizations --- Lagrange mesh versus Laguerre basis),

Three features organize the map.

\emph{Screening-amplified field ionization.} That widths grow when
screening strengthens at fixed field was observed at individual
parameter points already in Refs.~\cite{YuHo2005,LinHo2011}; the map
quantifies the law and its dramatic field dependence. From
$\lambda=0$ to $0.8$ the amplification factor is $\sim\!10^{4}$ at
$F=0.04$, $135$ at $F=0.06$, $22$ at $F=0.08$, and $9$ at $F=0.10$;
at $F=0.02$ the $\lambda=0$ width lies below the $10^{-10}$
resolution while the $\lambda=0.8$ width is $1.3\times10^{-2}$ ---
more than eight orders of magnitude. The asymmetry has a transparent
quasistatic origin: screening lifts the level, and the tunneling
exponent $\exp[-\tfrac{2}{3F}(2|E_r|)^{3/2}]$~\cite{
DamburgKolosov1976} reacts exponentially. This argument applies in
the sub-barrier regime $F\lesssim E_r^{2}/4$, which at $F=0.02$ means
$\lambda\lesssim0.3$; there it is quantitative --- between
$\lambda=0.2$ and $0.3$ the exponent predicts a growth of $196$
against a measured $96$ --- while beyond it the state is over-barrier
and the exponential serves only as a heuristic for the trend. The
practical reading for plasma spectroscopy: a field that is
spectroscopically harmless for the unscreened atom
($\Gamma<10^{-10}$) can dominate the line width entirely at strong
screening.

\emph{Dissolution frontier.} As an operational criterion for the
loss of spectroscopic identity we take $\Gamma=|E_r|$; any $O(1)$
ratio gives a similar curve. It is crossed inside the computed range
only for $F\geq0.06$: at $\lambda^{*}\approx0.78$, $0.73$, and
$0.68\;(\pm0.02$, linear interpolation on the $\Delta\lambda=0.05$
grid$)$ for $F=0.06$, $0.08$, and $0.10$. The frontier moves toward
weaker screening as the field grows, delimiting the region of the
$(F,\lambda)$ plane where a plasma-embedded atom retains a
resolvable line.

\emph{Shrinking perturbative regime.} The center panel of
Fig.~\ref{fig:map} isolates the field's contribution to the
position. At $\lambda=0$ the displacements are perturbative:
$-0.909\times10^{-3}$ at $F=0.02$ versus $-\tfrac94F^{2}=
-0.9\times10^{-3}$, and at $F=0.10$ the measured
$-27.4\times10^{-3}$ is bracketed by the second- and fourth-order
asymptotic estimates. Screening destroys this hierarchy rapidly. At
$F=0.02$ the displacement deepens by a factor $6.8$ between
$\lambda=0$ and $0.8$ --- but this is \emph{not} a growing
polarizability in the quadratic sense: halving the field to $F=0.01$
at $\lambda=0.8$ reduces the displacement by only a factor $2.24$
(quadratic response would give $4$), so even the weakest fields
considered are beyond the perturbative regime at strong screening.
At strong field the displacement is non-monotonic in $\lambda$,
peaking at $-32.3\times10^{-3}$ near $\lambda\approx0.45$ for
$F=0.10$ and retreating to $-18.9\times10^{-3}$ at $\lambda=0.8$;
quadrupling the field from $F=0.02$ to $0.04$ at $\lambda=0.8$
increases it by only $1.8\times$. Screening shrinks the perturbative
Stark regime, and beyond it the dominant field response is the
width, not the shift.

\subsection{The $n=2$ doublet across $E=0$}

In the $m=0$ sector the $n=2$ level carries exactly two components,
labeled by the parabolic quantum numbers $k=\pm1$ of the $\lambda=0$
limit; since screening destroys parabolic separability, $k$ is used
throughout as a continuation label of the pole trajectory, not as a
quantum number. At $F=0.02$ --- five times the classical ionization
field of $n=2$ --- both components are broad resonances already at
$\lambda=0$: $E=-0.206682-i\,0.030393$ ($k=-1$, downhill) and
$-0.088984-i\,0.015446$ ($k=+1$, uphill), the downhill component
ionizing twice as fast and both positions deviating from
second-order perturbation theory by $+0.012$ and $+0.010$.
Figure~\ref{fig:manifold} follows the doublet in $\lambda$ up to
$0.30$ ($\theta_c$-spreads below $10^{-5}$ except the two broadest
points, worst $1\times10^{-4}$). Screening lifts both components at
nearly the same initial rate (the slope
$dE/d\lambda|_{0}=\langle e^{-\lambda r}\rangle\to1$ is
state-independent), so the splitting survives while the doublet as a
whole rises. At $\lambda\approx0.11$ the uphill component crosses
$E_r=0$. For $F\neq0$ this is not a spectral threshold --- the Stark
Hamiltonian's spectrum covers the real line, and the scaled
operator's eigenvalues continue analytically in the
parameters~\cite{HerbstSimon1978} --- so the smooth passage,
confirmed numerically with no anomaly in position or width, is the
expected analytic continuation. The physical content is that the
state \emph{retains} narrow, identifiable resonance character on the
positive-energy side, reaching $E_r=+0.0837$, $\Gamma=0.070$ at
$\lambda=0.30$: the analog, for an excited Stark component, of the
positive-energy resonances of the field-free screened
atom~\cite{companionNR} and of the above-threshold ground-state
resonances tabulated at extreme screening in Ref.~\cite{YuHo2005}. The
downhill width grows from $0.061$ to $0.160$ over the same range,
and the width ratio $\Gamma_{-1}/\Gamma_{+1}$ drifts from $1.97$ to
$2.29$. Coverage here is deliberately scoped: one field value, one
manifold; the systematic excited-manifold survey is a natural
continuation.

\subsection{Physical scope}

The computed ranges correspond to realizable plasma conditions: for
electron densities $10^{21}$--$10^{22}\;\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ at
temperatures of $1$--$2$~eV, the electron Debye screening parameter
is $\lambda\approx0.2$--$0.5$~a.u.\ and the Holtsmark normal field
$F_{0}=2.603\,e\,n_e^{2/3}$ is $\approx0.007$--$0.034$~a.u., so the
grid covers the regime where the two perturbations genuinely
co-occur. In a plasma the ``static field'' is of course the
quasistatic ion microfield, a stochastic quantity distributed around
$F_{0}$~\cite{Griem1974}; the present uniform-field map supplies
precisely the resonance parameters $E_r(F,\lambda)$,
$\Gamma(F,\lambda)$ that enter a microfield average, which is the
standard route from Stark parameters to line shapes.

\section{Conclusions}

We have presented a coupled-channel complex-scaling method for atoms
under combined Debye screening and a static electric field ---
validated to ten digits against the classical Stark benchmark, to
$2.4\times10^{-7}$ against an independent engine in the screening
limit, and verified at run time against exact identities --- and used
it to compute what is, to our knowledge, the most extensive resonance
map of the combined problem to date, together with the $n=2$ doublet
continued across $E=0$. The map's physics organizes into a strongly
asymmetric competition: screening amplifies field ionization
exponentially (eight orders of magnitude at weak field), the
spectroscopic-identity frontier recedes to weaker screening as the
field grows, the perturbative Stark regime shrinks with screening
until the width dominates the field response, and excited Stark
components are pushed across the field-free threshold, persisting as
positive-energy resonances.

The method extends without structural change to $m\neq0$ sectors,
higher manifolds, and other screening functions (only the diagonal
potential blocks change); the exact-basis channels keep the matrices
small enough that a full map is a matter of minutes on a laptop,
which is what makes systematic coverage --- rather than isolated
points --- routine. At mesh-level resolutions or for many-electron
generalizations the same block structure is where contour-integral
eigensolvers would become the natural tool. All scripts,
certification batteries, and machine-readable grids are distributed
with the companion programs and will be archived with a persistent
identifier at publication.
