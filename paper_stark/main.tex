\title{The Stark problem in a Debye plasma: a certified coupled-channel
map of field--screening competition}

\author{C.~D.~Reinoso}
\email{cesardanielrrch@gmail.com}
\affiliation{Facultad de Ciencias, Escuela Superior Polit\'ecnica de
Chimborazo (ESPOCH), Riobamba, Ecuador}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
A hydrogen-like atom in a static electric field and a Debye plasma
experiences two competing ionization mechanisms: field ionization
through the Stark barrier and bound-state dissolution by screening. The
combined problem is non-separable, and the published record consists of
isolated parameter points. We solve it with a coupled-channel
complex-scaled method whose radial channels carry the exact Laguerre
basis of the unperturbed problem, coupled only by the dipole selection
rule $\ell\to\ell\pm1$: the resulting block-tridiagonal complex-symmetric
pencils reach the classical pure-Stark benchmark at $F=0.1$ a.u.\
($E=-0.527418175-0.007269057i$) with fewer than $10^{3}$ degrees of
freedom and geometric convergence in the channel cutoff, and every
assembly is certified at run time against the exact hydrogen spectrum
--- a discipline that caught a factor-of-two kinetic-energy defect
masked by an apparently convergent spurious state during development,
and that directly addresses the known sensitivity of complex-rotation
width calculations to matrix-element precision. On this basis we
compute the first systematic resonance map of the combined problem: a
$5\times17$ grid in the $(F,\lambda)$ plane for the ground state and
the $m=0$ Stark doublet of $n=2$ under screening. The map quantifies
a strong asymmetry in the field--screening competition ---
\emph{screening-catalyzed field ionization}: at $F=0.02$ the width
grows by more than eight orders of magnitude across the screening
range, while at $F=0.10$ it grows by one --- locates the dissolution
frontier $\Gamma=|E_r|$, and reveals a non-monotonic field shift at
strong field. The $n=2$ doublet is continued smoothly through the
ionization threshold: screening pushes the uphill ($k=+1$) Stark
component to positive energies at $\lambda\approx0.11$, where it
survives as an above-threshold resonance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Stark effect in hydrogen and the screened Coulomb (Yukawa--Debye)
potential are two classical single-parameter problems whose resonance
structure is known to high precision: the pure Stark resonances from
complex-dilation and resummation
methods~\cite{Herbst1979,Kolosov1987,Telnov1989,Jentschura2001}, and
the screened-Coulomb bound states and critical screenings from decades
of plasma-physics work~\cite{RogersGraboskeHarwood1970,
delValleNader2018}. Their combination --- an atom in a plasma
subjected to a static field, the generic situation in dense-plasma
spectroscopy --- is qualitatively richer: the field tilts the
potential and opens a Stark barrier, screening lifts and delocalizes
the bound states, and the two ionization mechanisms compete. The
combined problem is non-separable (screening breaks the parabolic
separability of the Stark problem), and the published record consists
of a small number of parameter points: the pioneering ground-state
calculations of Yu and Ho~\cite{YuHo2005}, the complex-scaled
Lagrange-mesh study of Lin and Ho~\cite{LinHo2011} covering ground and
selected excited states, and bound-state-level work at weak
fields~\cite{BaharSoylu2014,DasGhoshal2025}. A systematic map of the
$(F,\lambda)$ plane --- positions, widths, critical curves, and the
fate of the excited Stark manifolds under screening --- has not been
available.

This paper supplies such a map, built on two methodological choices.
First, instead of a two-dimensional mesh, we exploit the dipole
selection rule: the field couples only adjacent angular channels, so
the problem is a block-tridiagonal radial system in which each channel
carries the \emph{exact} orthonormal Laguerre basis of the
unperturbed radial problem~\cite{companionNR}, and the whole pencil is
complex-scaled analytically. Convergence in the channel cutoff is
geometric, and the classical pure-Stark benchmark is reproduced to ten
digits with fewer than a thousand degrees of freedom --- two orders of
magnitude below mesh requirements. Second, every assembly is certified
at run time against exact identities (the unperturbed hydrogen
spectrum, channel overlaps), a discipline inherited from the companion
programs~\cite{companionNR,companionA}. This is not decorative: an
early build of the present code carried a factor-of-two kinetic-energy
defect that produced a \emph{convergent, rotation-stable} spurious
state at a plausible energy --- caught in seconds by the
exact-spectrum certification, not by convergence studies. Width
calculations by complex rotation are notoriously sensitive to
matrix-element precision; run-time certification against exact
identities is our systematic answer to that sensitivity.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

In the $m=0$ sector the Hamiltonian of a hydrogen-like atom in a
Debye plasma and a static field $F\hat z$,
\begin{equation}
H=-\tfrac12\nabla^{2}-\frac{Z e^{-\lambda r}}{r}+F z,
\end{equation}
acts on channel wave functions $u_{\ell}(r)$ coupled only by the
dipole matrix elements of $z=r\cos\theta$:
$\langle\ell+1,0|\cos\theta|\ell,0\rangle=
(\ell+1)/\sqrt{(2\ell+1)(2\ell+3)}$. Each channel is expanded in the
orthonormal generalized Laguerre basis of
Ref.~\cite{companionNR}, with a common scale and quadrature grid
across channels so that the inter-channel dipole blocks are exact
contractions. Complex scaling $r\to re^{i\theta_{c}}$ acts
analytically on every block --- kinetic terms scale by
$e^{-2i\theta_{c}}$, Coulombic parts by $e^{-i\theta_{c}}$, the
linear Stark coupling by $e^{+i\theta_{c}}$, and the screening factor
enters the quadrature at complex argument~\cite{AguilarCombes1971,
BalslevCombes1971,Herbst1979,Ho1983,Moiseyev1998} --- giving a
block-tridiagonal complex-symmetric pencil. Resonances are identified
by $\theta_{c}$-stability between two rotation angles, and every
tabulated value carries the maximum spread over a grid of two channel
cutoffs and two basis sizes.

Certification: at $F=\lambda=0$ the assembled pencil must reproduce
the exact hydrogen levels $-1/2n^{2}$; we require agreement below
$10^{-9}$ on the lowest five levels at every build, together with
channel-overlap identities at the $10^{-12}$ level. At $F=0.1$,
$\lambda=0$ the method gives
$E=-0.527418175-0.007269057i$ with geometric convergence in the
channel cutoff ($4\times10^{-5}\to5\times10^{-10}$ over
$\ell_{\max}=4\to16$) at $780$ degrees of freedom, in agreement with
the classical complex-dilation value~\cite{Kolosov1987,Telnov1989,
Jentschura2001}; in the opposite limit $F=0$, $\lambda=0.25$ the
$\ell=1$ block reproduces the independently triple-verified $2p$
resonance of the one-dimensional engine~\cite{companionNR}. A remark
on solver scale: at these dimensions dense complex diagonalization
computes each map point in seconds, so contour-integral eigensolvers,
useful at mesh scales, are unnecessary here.

Three features organize the map.

\emph{Screening-catalyzed field ionization.} At fixed field, the
width grows monotonically --- and enormously --- with screening: from
$\lambda=0$ to $\lambda=0.8$ the amplification factor is
$\sim\!10^{4}$ at $F=0.04$, $135$ at $F=0.06$, $22$ at $F=0.08$, and
$9$ at $F=0.10$; at $F=0.02$ the $\lambda=0$ width lies below the
$10^{-10}$ resolution while the $\lambda=0.8$ width is
$1.3\times10^{-2}$ --- more than eight orders of magnitude. The
asymmetry has a transparent quasistatic origin: screening lifts the
level, and the tunneling exponent
$\exp[-\tfrac{2}{3F}(2|E_r|)^{3/2}]$ reacts exponentially. At
$F=0.02$ this exponent alone predicts a growth of $1.7\times10^{7}$
between $\lambda=0.2$ and $0.8$, within an order of magnitude of the
measured $2.1\times10^{6}$, the residue being the slowly varying
prefactor. The practical reading for plasma spectroscopy: a field
that is spectroscopically harmless for the unscreened atom
($\Gamma<10^{-10}$) can dominate the line width entirely at strong
screening.

\emph{Dissolution frontier.} The half-width criterion
$\Gamma=|E_r|$ --- beyond which the state no longer supports a
resolvable line --- is crossed inside the computed range only for
$F\geq0.06$: at $\lambda^{*}\approx0.78$, $0.73$, and $0.68$ for
$F=0.06$, $0.08$, and $0.10$. The frontier moves toward weaker
screening as the field grows, delimiting the region of the
$(F,\lambda)$ plane where a plasma-embedded atom retains
spectroscopic identity.

\emph{Non-monotonic field shift.} The center panel of
Fig.~\ref{fig:map} isolates the field's contribution to the position.
At $\lambda=0$ the shifts are perturbative:
$-0.909\times10^{-3}$ at $F=0.02$ versus $-\tfrac94F^{2}=
-0.9\times10^{-3}$, and at $F=0.10$ the measured
$-27.4\times10^{-3}$ is bracketed by the second- and fourth-order
asymptotic estimates. As $\lambda$ grows at $F=0.02$ the shift
deepens by a factor $6.8$ --- the screened atom is softer, its
effective polarizability larger. At strong field, however, the shift
is non-monotonic: at $F=0.10$ it peaks at $-32.5\times10^{-3}$ near
$\lambda\approx0.45$ and then \emph{retreats} to
$-18.9\times10^{-3}$ at $\lambda=0.8$. Quadrupling the field from
$F=0.02$ to $0.04$ at $\lambda=0.8$ increases the shift by only
$1.8\times$ instead of $4\times$: screening shrinks the perturbative
Stark regime, and beyond it the dominant field response is the width,
not the shift.

\subsection{The $n=2$ doublet through the threshold}

In the $m=0$ sector the $n=2$ level carries exactly two parabolic
components, $k=\pm1$. At $F=0.02$ --- five times the classical
ionization field of $n=2$ --- both are broad resonances already at
$\lambda=0$: $E=-0.206682-i\,0.030395$ ($k=-1$, downhill) and
$-0.088984-i\,0.015445$ ($k=+1$, uphill), the downhill component
ionizing twice as fast, and both positions deviating from
second-order perturbation theory by $+0.012$ and $+0.010$ ---
strongly nonperturbative. Figure~\ref{fig:manifold} follows the
doublet in $\lambda$. Screening lifts both components at almost the
same initial rate (the slope $dE/d\lambda|_{0}=\langle
e^{-\lambda r}\rangle\to1$ is state-independent), so the splitting
survives while the doublet as a whole is pushed toward the continuum.
At $\lambda\approx0.11$ the uphill component crosses $E_r=0$ ---
and nothing dramatic happens to it: it continues smoothly as an
above-threshold resonance, reaching $E_r=+0.0837$,
$\Gamma=0.070$ at $\lambda=0.30$, the same phenomenon as the
positive-energy resonances of the field-free screened
atom~\cite{companionNR}, here reached by a Stark component under
screening continuation. The downhill width grows from $0.061$ to
$0.160$ over the same range, and the width ratio
$\Gamma_{-1}/\Gamma_{+1}$ drifts from $1.97$ to $2.29$.

\section{Conclusions}

We have presented a certified coupled-channel complex-scaling method
for atoms in the combined presence of Debye screening and a static
electric field, validated to ten digits against the classical Stark
benchmark and to $2.4\times10^{-7}$ against an independent engine in
the screening limit, and used it to compute the first systematic
resonance map of the combined problem. The map's physics organizes
into a strongly asymmetric competition: screening catalyzes field
ionization exponentially (eight orders of magnitude at weak field),
the spectroscopic-identity frontier $\Gamma=|E_r|$ recedes to weaker
screening as the field grows, the perturbative Stark regime shrinks
with screening until the width, not the shift, is the dominant field
response, and excited Stark components are pushed through the
ionization threshold, surviving as above-threshold resonances.

The method extends without structural change to $m\neq0$ sectors,
higher manifolds, and other screening functions (only the diagonal
potential blocks change); the exact-basis channels keep the pencils
small enough that a full map is a matter of minutes on a laptop,
which is what makes systematic coverage --- rather than isolated
points --- routine. At mesh-level resolutions or for many-electron
generalizations the same block structure is where contour-integral
eigensolvers would become the natural tool. All scripts,
certification batteries, and machine-readable grids are distributed
with the companion programs.
