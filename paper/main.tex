\begin{abstract}
Three ingredients distinguish this study of screened Coulomb
(Debye--H\"uckel) spectra. First, the radial Schr\"odinger problem is
discretized in a generalized-Laguerre basis whose orthonormality is
certified rather than assumed: the quadrature behind every matrix element
must reproduce the overlap identity at run time. Second, eigenvalues are
extracted with FEASTci, our from-scratch implementation of the FEAST
contour-integration algorithm, whose contour here is a small circle placed
off the real axis around an anticipated resonance: computing every
eigenvalue inside a prescribed region is exactly what resonance search
under complex scaling requires, a pairing, to our knowledge, not
exploited before. Third,
every reported quantity is validated externally:
hydrogen bound states reproduce the exact spectrum to $3\times10^{-10}$
Hartree, critical screening parameters (the values at which a level ceases
to be bound) agree with published values to within $2\times10^{-9}$
inverse Bohr radii (eight significant digits) for $\ell \ge 1$, the
$\ell = 0$ values falling low by a few parts in $10^{4}$ through a bias we
identify and quantify, and near-threshold resonance positions and
half-widths reproduce twelve-digit benchmarks to $3\times10^{-11}$
Hartree; in passing they settle a discrepancy between two published
datasets. The implementation is open
source.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
FEASTci; FEAST eigensolver; contour integration; Laguerre basis;
complex scaling; screened Coulomb potential; resonances.
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A charge embedded in a plasma does not feel the bare Coulomb interaction:
the surrounding mobile charges screen it, and in the simplest
Debye--H\"uckel picture the interaction becomes the Yukawa form
$V(r) = -Z e^{-\lambda r}/r$, with $r$ the distance from the nucleus, $Z$
the charge number of the nucleus or central ion ($Z=1$ throughout this
paper), and $\lambda$ the screening parameter, the inverse Debye length,
so that $1/\lambda$ is the range beyond which the nuclear charge is
hidden~\cite{debye1923theorie,bard2022electrochemical}. The spectral
consequences are severe: the infinite hydrogenic series collapses to a
finite number of bound levels (states of negative energy, held by the
potential), and the remaining states shift and eventually dissolve into
the continuum (the unbound states above the threshold $E=0$) as $\lambda$
grows. Screened atomic systems remain an active subject, from confinement
and pressure response to nonlinear optics and the bound--continuum
boundary in dense plasmas
\cite{Mukherjee2021,Zhou2023epjd,He2023,Yan2024}.

A level that crosses the threshold need not disappear. For states with
$\ell \ge 1$, where $\ell$ is the orbital angular-momentum quantum number
and the repulsive centrifugal term $\ell(\ell+1)/2r^2$ of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:radial}) raises a barrier that can still trap the electron,
the bound state turns into a resonance: a state that leaks away at a
finite rate and carries the complex energy $E = E_r - i\Gamma/2$, with
$E_r$ its position, $\Gamma$ its width and $1/\Gamma$ its lifetime. The
standard computational route to such states is complex scaling
(Sec.~\ref{sec:radial}), which exposes them as isolated square-integrable
eigenstates \cite{Moiseyev2011} and remains the workhorse for screened
systems, from single-particle potentials \cite{Jiao2021} to doubly excited
states of helium in dense plasmas \cite{Dong2024}. After discretization
the problem is always the same: find a few complex eigenvalues of a large
non-Hermitian matrix inside a region of the complex plane known
approximately in advance.

That formulation matches the design goal of FEAST, an \emph{algorithm}
rather than a single fixed piece of software, introduced by
Polizzi~\cite{Polizzi2009}: it recasts interior-eigenvalue extraction as a
contour-integral spectral projector, returning all eigenpairs enclosed by
a chosen contour through numerical quadrature of the resolvent
\cite{Polizzi2009,Kestyn2016}. Ours, FEASTci (FEAST by contour
integration, Sec.~\ref{sec:feast}), is written from scratch and adapted
for resonance search under complex scaling. Contour and rational-filter
eigensolvers are an active subject in numerical linear algebra
\cite{Brennan2023}, yet to our knowledge FEAST has not been used for the
resonance spectroscopy of screened Coulomb systems, the gap this paper
fills.

The discretization deserves the same care. We expand the reduced radial
function in a basis of generalized Laguerre functions
(Sec.~\ref{sec:basis}) orthonormal in $L^2(0,\infty)$ by
construction. Such bases have a long history in atomic structure, from
Sturmian expansions \cite{Rotenberg1970} to current Dirac-equation studies
\cite{Wu2025}, with pseudospectral variants in active use for confined and
scaled systems \cite{Zhu2020}. The basis itself is standard; what is new
here is that the quadrature behind every matrix element is certified a
posteriori through the overlap identity (Sec.~\ref{sec:elements}).

The hydrogen atom appears in a single role, as the validation benchmark:
its spectrum is known in closed form, $E_n = -1/(2n^2)$ with
$n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ the principal quantum number. Hartree atomic units
(a.u.) are used throughout, as defined in Tab.~\ref{tab:notation}, which
also collects the main symbols and the unit each one carries. That exact
spectrum calibrates basis, solver and complex-scaling machinery before
they face the screened problem, where no exact solution exists.

\subsection{Radial problem and complex scaling}
\label{sec:radial}

In Hartree atomic units the reduced radial equation for a spherically
symmetric potential reads
\begin{equation}
\left[-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^2}{dr^2}
      +\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2r^2}+V(r)\right]u_{n\ell}(r)
      = E_{n\ell}\,u_{n\ell}(r),
\qquad u(0)=0,\quad u(\infty)=0,
\label{eq:radial}
\end{equation}
with $V(r)=-Z/r$ for hydrogen and $V(r)=-Z e^{-\lambda r}/r$ for the
Debye-screened case, and $u_{n\ell}$ the reduced radial wave function,
that is $r$ times the radial amplitude, normalized by
$\int_0^\infty u_{n\ell}^2\,dr = 1$, of energy $E_{n\ell}$.
Under the complex-scaling transformation
$r \to r e^{i\theta}$ the Hamiltonian becomes the complex symmetric
operator
\begin{equation}
H(\theta) = e^{-2i\theta}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^2}{dr^2}
          +\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2r^2}\right]
          - Z e^{-i\theta}\,\frac{e^{-\lambda r e^{i\theta}}}{r}.
\label{eq:cs}
\end{equation}
Bound eigenvalues of $H(\theta)$, with $\theta>0$ the rotation angle,
coincide with those of $H(0)$; continuum branches rotate by $-2\theta$
around their thresholds; resonances become isolated complex eigenvalues
$E_r - i\Gamma/2$, stationary in $\theta$ once
uncovered \cite{Moiseyev2011}.

\subsection{Orthonormal Laguerre basis}
\label{sec:basis}

The scale parameter $\beta>0$, in inverse Bohr radii, fixes the decay rate
$e^{-\beta r}$ of every basis function and so the radial extent covered
well: small $\beta$ favours diffuse states, large $\beta$ states near the
origin. With the dimensionless variable $x = 2\beta r$, define for
$j = 0, 1, \dots, N-1$
\begin{equation}
\varphi_j(r) = \sqrt{2\beta}\,
   \sqrt{\frac{j!}{(j+2\ell+2)!}}\;
   x^{\ell+1} e^{-x/2} L_j^{(2\ell+2)}(x),
\label{eq:basis}
\end{equation}
where $L_j^{(\alpha)}$ is the generalized Laguerre polynomial of degree
$j$ and parameter $\alpha$, here $\alpha = 2\ell+2$. The weight
$x^{2\ell+2}e^{-x}$ generated by the product of two such functions is
exactly the orthogonality weight of $L_j^{(2\ell+2)}$, so the set is
orthonormal in $L^2(0,\infty)$,
\begin{equation}
\langle \varphi_i \,|\, \varphi_j \rangle
 = \int_0^\infty \varphi_i(r)\,\varphi_j(r)\,dr = \delta_{ij},
\label{eq:orthonormal}
\end{equation}
without any overlap matrix. Each $\varphi_j$ vanishes as $r^{\ell+1}$ at
the origin, the regularity required of $u_{n\ell}$, and decays as
$e^{-\beta r}$. They are evaluated with the three-term recurrence of the
orthonormalized polynomials, stable for the sizes used here ($N \le 320$).

\subsection{Matrix elements and certification}
\label{sec:elements}

Kinetic, centrifugal and Coulomb matrix elements reduce to integrals of
polynomials against $e^{-x}$ and are evaluated with a Gauss--Legendre rule
in the dimensionless variable $x$ on $(0, x_{\max})$, with
$x_{\max} = 4N + 24(\ell+1) + 120$ and $\max(3000, 16N)$ nodes, using the
derivative identity
$x\,dL_j^{(\alpha)}/dx = jL_j^{(\alpha)} - (j+\alpha)L_{j-1}^{(\alpha)}$
for the kinetic term. The screening factor $e^{-\lambda r}$ and its
complex-scaled counterpart in Eq.~(\ref{eq:cs}) enter the quadrature
directly. Rather than argue exactness we certify it: the same rule applied
to the overlap matrix $S_{ij}=\langle\varphi_i|\varphi_j\rangle$ of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:orthonormal}) must return the identity. The largest deviation
$\max_{ij}|S_{ij}-\delta_{ij}|$ (dimensionless) is evaluated whenever a
basis is constructed and the run is aborted if it exceeds $10^{-9}$; the
measured values grow slowly with $N$, from $1.5\times10^{-12}$ for the
$N=180$ basis of Tab.~\ref{tab:yukawa} to at most $9.7\times10^{-12}$ for
the $N=320$ basis of Tab.~\ref{tab:lambdac}, the largest built here, so
orthonormality is a measured property, not an assumption.

\subsection{FEASTci: our implementation of FEAST}
\label{sec:feast}

From now on $H$ is the $N \times N$ matrix representing the operator of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:cs}) in the basis of Eq.~(\ref{eq:basis}). FEAST is based on
the spectral projector $P$ onto the eigenspace of $H$ enclosed by a
contour $\mathcal{C}$ in the complex plane,
\begin{equation}
P = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}} (z - H)^{-1}\, dz .
\label{eq:projector}
\end{equation}
Applied to a block $Y \in \complexset^{N \times m_0}$ of $m_0$ random trial
vectors (real entries suffice for the real-interval variant; the
circular-contour variant draws complex entries), with $m_0$ exceeding the
number of enclosed eigenvalues, the projector is approximated by an
$n_c$-node Gauss--Legendre quadrature
$Q \approx \displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n_c} \omega_k (z_k - H)^{-1} Y$
(nodes $z_k$ on
$\mathcal{C}$, weights $\omega_k$), and the eigenproblem is solved in
$\mathrm{span}(Q)$ by a Rayleigh--Ritz step; a few subspace iterations
with this rational filter drive the residuals to machine precision
\cite{Polizzi2009}.

No external FEAST library is called: both variants are written from
scratch in the released code and validated against direct LAPACK
diagonalization. The real-interval variant follows Polizzi's
real-symmetric algorithm~\cite{Polizzi2009}. The circular-contour variant
adapts the non-Hermitian FEAST of Kestyn et al.~\cite{Kestyn2016} to the
resonance problem, keeping the single-sided construction common to
contour-integral eigensolvers for general matrices: the orthonormal basis
$W$ of $\mathrm{span}(Q)$, obtained by a QR factorization, yields the
reduced matrix $H_r = W^{\dagger} H(\theta) W$, with $W^{\dagger}$ the
conjugate transpose of $W$, so no left subspace and no biorthogonalization
are needed. The dense problem $H_r v = \lambda v$ is solved by LAPACK, the
Ritz values enclosed by $\mathcal{C}$ are selected, and the subspace
iteration stops when the largest residual
$\max_j \|H(\theta)\,x_j - \lambda_j x_j\|_2$ falls below $10^{-9}\Ha$.
The methodological contribution of this work is the placement of the
contour: detached from the real axis and centered on the
\emph{anticipated} resonance position of Eq.~(\ref{eq:cs}), so a small
circle isolates $E_r - i\Gamma/2$ while excluding the rotated continuum.

\section{Validation on the hydrogen atom}
\label{sec:validation}

Three independent instruments are compared throughout
(Tab.~\ref{tab:provenance}): the Laguerre-basis pipeline, direct LAPACK
diagonalization of the same matrices, and a finite-difference solver on a
uniform grid sharing no code with it. Figs.~\ref{fig:conv0}
and~\ref{fig:conv1} plot the worst-state error
$\max_n|\Delta E_n|$ in Ha, with $\Delta E_n = E_n - E_n^{\rm exact}$,
against the basis size $N$ for several scale parameters $\beta$ in
$a_0^{-1}$, over the six lowest $\ell=0$ and the five lowest $\ell=1$
eigenvalues respectively. With $N=150$ functions and $\beta = 1/6$ the
$\ell=0$ eigenvalues reproduce $E_n = -1/(2n^2)$ with errors between
$2.3\times10^{-10}\Ha$ ($n=1$) and $1.1\times10^{-12}\Ha$ ($n=6$); at the
same $N$ and $\beta$ the $\ell=1$ errors run from $2.6\times10^{-13}\Ha$
($n=2$) down to $1.2\times10^{-14}\Ha$ ($n=6$). The error decays
exponentially with $N$, as straight segments on the logarithmic scale,
until the curves flatten at the accuracy floor set by double-precision
arithmetic and the conditioning of the discretized operator. That floor is
channel dependent, about $10^{-10}\Ha$ for $\ell=0$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:conv0})
and three to four orders lower for $\ell=1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:conv1}); that
signature distinguishes a spectral basis from the algebraic
$O(\Delta r^2)$ convergence of the finite-difference reference.

\section{Screened Coulomb potential}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Bound states}
\label{sec:bound}

States carry the spectroscopic label $n\ell$, in which s, p, d denote
$\ell = 0, 1, 2$: 1s is the ground state ($n=1$, $\ell=0$), 2p has $n=2$,
$\ell=1$, and 3d has $n=3$, $\ell=2$.
Laguerre and finite-difference energies (Tab.~\ref{tab:yukawa}) agree at
the level of the finite-difference discretization error, about
$2\times10^{-6}\Ha$ for the radial step $\Delta r = 0.004\,a_0$, while the
basis values are converged well beyond that: the entries of
Tab.~\ref{tab:yukawa} with published high-precision
counterparts~\cite{delValle2018,Alhaidari2008,Gebremedhin2023} agree with
them to $3\times10^{-10}\Ha$ or better, and a run with a larger basis
($N=320$, $\beta=0.05\,a_0^{-1}$) reproduces the twelve-digit J-matrix
(algebraic scattering) 2p value~\cite{Alhaidari2008} at
$\lambda=0.2\,a_0^{-1}$ to $1.5\times10^{-12}\Ha$.

\subsection{Critical screening parameters}
\label{sec:critical}

The critical screening parameter $\lambda_c$, the value of $\lambda$ at
which a level stops being bound, was located by bisection on the number of
negative eigenvalues, a criterion immune to the discretized continuum
states hovering near $E=0$ in any finite $L^2$ basis.
Tab.~\ref{tab:lambdac} compares the results with five decades of steadily
refined literature values
\cite{Rogers1970,Diaz1991,delValle2018,JiaoCrit2021,Gebremedhin2023}: for
$\ell \ge 1$ ours agree with the Lagrange-mesh (orthogonal-collocation)
determinations~\cite{delValle2018} to within $2\times10^{-9}\,a_0^{-1}$,
the first eight significant digits coinciding in every case. For
$\ell = 0$ the procedure carries a systematic deficit of a few parts in
$10^{4}$: an $\ell=0$ (s-wave) binding energy vanishes quadratically in
$\lambda_c - \lambda$, so the threshold state becomes arbitrarily diffuse
and the bound-state count flips slightly before the true critical point,
while states with a centrifugal barrier remain localized at threshold. An
$E(\lambda) \to 0$ extrapolation removing that bias is left for a
follow-up study.

\subsection{Resonances}
\label{sec:resonances}

Above $\lambda_c(2\mathrm{p}) = 0.220216806\,a_0^{-1}$ the 2p level
crosses into the continuum and survives as a shape resonance, trapped
behind its centrifugal barrier by the shape of the effective potential
alone. Fig.~\ref{fig:trajectory} shows the spectrum of the
complex-scaled Hamiltonian for $\ell=1$ at $\lambda = 0.25\,a_0^{-1}$ as the
rotation angle grows from $0.10$ to $0.60$, the shading keyed to $\theta$:
the discretized continuum rotates downward as a fan of rays while one
eigenvalue detaches and stalls. That $\theta$-independence distinguishes a
physical pole from a continuum artifact; the stationary point, marked by
the star, is the resonance; over the last rotation steps, magnified in
the inset, the distance between consecutive eigenvalue positions
contracts to below $10^{-14}\Ha$. The same
point is recomputed with the circular-contour FEASTci variant: a contour
of radius $8\times10^{-4}\Ha$ centered on the trajectory estimate returns a
single enclosed eigenvalue agreeing to better than $2\times10^{-10}\Ha$,
with residuals below $10^{-9}\Ha$. Repeating with $N = 150$ and $200$
basis functions and with $\beta = 0.30$ and $0.45\,a_0^{-1}$ leaves every
quoted digit unchanged.

The decisive test is external: twelve-digit complex-rotation (the
complex-scaling method of Sec.~\ref{sec:radial}) and J-matrix benchmarks
exist for near-threshold Yukawa
resonances \cite{Bylicki2007,Alhaidari2008}, and Tab.~\ref{tab:resbench}
confronts them with the present scheme. For 2p and 3d we reproduce the
J-matrix values~\cite{Alhaidari2008} to $10^{-11}\Ha$ in position and
$3\times10^{-11}\Ha$ in half-width. For 3p the two sources disagree at the fourth digit (the
J-matrix tabulation~\cite{Alhaidari2008} gives
$E_r = 3.096253670542\times10^{-5}$,
$\Gamma/2 = 4.640977622365\times10^{-6}$); our value sides with the
original complex-rotation data of Bylicki, Stach\'ow, Karwowski and
Mukherjee~\cite{Bylicki2007}, agreeing to about $5\times10^{-10}\Ha$.
These comparisons validate the complex-scaled matrix elements and the
$\theta$-trajectory extraction end to end, against calculations that share
neither code nor method with ours.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

An explicitly orthonormal Laguerre basis with certified quadrature,
combined with the FEASTci contour-integration eigensolver, converges
exponentially on hydrogen and reproduces direct diagonalization at machine
precision. On the Debye-screened problem it gives critical screening
parameters accurate to $2\times10^{-9}\,a_0^{-1}$ (eight significant
digits) for $\ell \ge 1$, near-threshold resonances matching the
twelve-digit benchmarks to $3\times10^{-11}\Ha$, and the
full 2p trajectory from threshold into the broad-resonance regime; where
two published datasets disagree, our method indicates which to prefer.

The methodological point is the pairing itself, and it carries over to
exponential-cosine screening, two-channel problems and the Stark problem,
where resonance positions are likewise known in advance.
