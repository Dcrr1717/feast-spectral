\title{Stark resonances under exponential-cosine screening: through-threshold
maps for the quantum-plasma hydrogen atom}

\author{C.~D.~Reinoso}
\email{cesardanielrrch@gmail.com}
\affiliation{Facultad de Ciencias, Escuela Superior Polit\'ecnica de
Chimborazo (ESPOCH), Riobamba, Ecuador}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
The exponential-cosine screened Coulomb (ECSC) potential of dense
quantum plasmas differs from Debye screening in one structural
respect: its tail oscillates. We compute the resonance spectrum of
the hydrogen atom under ECSC screening combined with a static
electric field, using a coupled-channel complex-scaling method whose
screened blocks cost nothing beyond the Debye machinery --- the ECSC
potential is the half-sum of two Yukawa potentials at complex
screening parameters, which also yields the analytic rotation bound
$\theta_c<\pi/4$. Three results organize the physics. At zero field,
parametric continuation answers a question left open in the
literature: between the critical screening
$\lambda_c=0.7205$ and the birth of tail shape resonances at
$\lambda\approx0.90$, the ground-state pole merges into the rotated
continuum --- consistent with the conjectured virtual-state scenario
--- while the $2p$ pole detaches at $\lambda_c(2p)=0.1482$ as a
genuine resonance, rotation-stable to $10^{-13}$. With the field on,
we map the ground resonance on a $5\times19$ grid of the
$(F,\lambda)$ plane: at every field strength the resonance crosses
$E_r=0$ near $\lambda^{*}\approx0.68$, almost independently of the
field, and continues smoothly as a positive-energy resonance --- a regime with no Debye analog in the
same parameter range, reached here because the field keeps the pole
isolated where the field-free pole dissolves. Finally, comparing
ECSC and Debye screening at matched binding energy isolates the
tail effect on ionization: the oscillatory tail suppresses the
field-ionization width by roughly a factor of two at weak field,
the suppression weakening as the field strengthens and the barrier
peak moves inward.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Screening in a dense, degenerate electron gas is not exponential.
Quantum hydrodynamic treatments of test-charge screening in quantum
plasmas~\cite{ShuklaEliasson2008} lead to the exponential-cosine
screened Coulomb (ECSC) potential,
\begin{equation}
V(r)=-\frac{Z}{r}\,e^{-\lambda r}\cos(\lambda r),
\label{eq:ecsc}
\end{equation}
whose study as a model interaction long predates that
derivation~\cite{LamVarshni1972}. The oscillating factor is not a
cosmetic difference from the Debye--H\"uckel form: the potential
changes sign, its tail alternates between attraction and repulsion,
and this modifies both the bound spectrum --- critical screening
parameters differ substantially~\cite{JiaoCritical2021} --- and the
resonance structure. Field-free ECSC resonances have recently been
mapped with high precision by the complex-scaling pseudospectral
work of Jiao and collaborators~\cite{JiaoHo2021}, who located the
loss of the ground state at $\lambda_c=0.720524$ and the emergence
of well-defined $s$-wave shape resonances at
$\lambda_{r+}=0.90035$, and who explicitly note that the behavior in
the intermediate window is unexplained, conjecturing conversion into
virtual states. Perturbative field responses (polarizabilities and
hyperpolarizabilities) of ECSC-screened atoms are likewise
known~\cite{PRE2023polarizabilities}, and the one nonperturbative
Stark calculation under ECSC screening --- complex-scaled resonance
energies and widths at selected parameter points --- was carried out
by Wang, Chen, and Guo~\cite{WangChenGuo2019}. What does not exist
is systematic coverage: a resonance map of the combined
field--screening plane, the fate of excited Stark manifolds, or a
controlled comparison against Debye screening that isolates what the
oscillating tail actually does to field ionization. This paper
supplies the three.

The tool is the coupled-channel complex-scaling method of our Debye
companion study~\cite{companionD}, and the extension to ECSC
screening is free: Eq.~(\ref{eq:ecsc}) is the half-sum of two Yukawa
potentials at complex screening parameters $\lambda(1\pm i)$, so
every screened matrix element reuses the Debye code path at complex
argument, and the identity yields at once the analytic bound
$\theta_c<\pi/4$ on the rotation angle. All runs are verified
against exact limits at run time, and the Yukawa mode of the
present engine reproduces the companion engine to
$2\times10^{-16}$.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

The $m=0$ coupled-channel complex-scaling machinery is that of
Ref.~\cite{companionD}: each angular channel carries the orthonormal
generalized-Laguerre radial basis with common scale $\beta=0.35$,
$n_b=70$ functions per channel and $\ell_{\max}=14$ (1050 degrees of
freedom) at production settings, a shared 3000-point Gauss--Legendre
quadrature, dipole blocks
$(\ell+1)/\sqrt{(2\ell+1)(2\ell+3)}$ coupling adjacent channels, and
analytic scaling of every operator~\cite{AguilarCombes1971,
BalslevCombes1971,Ho1983,Reinhardt1982,Moiseyev1998}; the Stark term
is covered by the Herbst--Simon theory~\cite{HerbstSimon1978,
Herbst1979}. The single ECSC-specific element is the screened
factor: writing $e^{-\lambda r}\cos\lambda r=\tfrac12
[e^{-\mu_+r}+e^{-\mu_-r}]$ with $\mu_\pm=\lambda(1\pm i)$, the
complex-scaled screening enters as two Yukawa evaluations at
$\mu_\pm re^{i\theta_c}$. Decay of these factors requires
$\mathrm{Re}(\mu_\pm e^{i\theta_c})>0$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
\theta_c<\pi/4,
\end{equation}
a hard analytic cap (enforced in the code) comfortably above the
production angles $\theta_c=0.18/0.28$ (retry $0.30/0.42$).
Resonances are identified exactly as in Ref.~\cite{companionD}:
parametric continuation in $\lambda$ with the search window
predicted from the $F=0$ bound curve plus the previous complex
field shift, acceptance by two-angle agreement with the modulus
difference recorded as the spread, and $\theta_c$-trajectories
computed for the least favorable points. Uncertainties quoted are
these spreads --- error estimates calibrated against the anchors
below, not bounds.

Verification. The hydrogen limit reproduces $-1/2n^{2}$ to
$7\times10^{-11}$ (a failed check aborts every run); the
$\lambda=0$ row of the production map reproduces the pure-Stark
anchors of Ref.~\cite{companionD} (themselves validated to ten
digits against the classical Stark values~\cite{DamburgKolosov1976,
Kolosov1987,Jentschura2001}); the small-$\lambda$ expansion of the
ground level follows the exact law $E_{1s}=-\tfrac12+\lambda
-\lambda^{3}\langle r^{2}\rangle/3+O(\lambda^{4})$ --- the
$\lambda^{2}$ term is absent for ECSC, unlike Debye screening ---
to the expected order (deviation $1.2\times10^{-8}$ at
$\lambda=0.01$); and our critical screening
$\lambda_c(1s)=0.72027$ agrees with the high-precision value
$0.720524$ of Ref.~\cite{JiaoCritical2021} within the
threshold-diffusivity bias of a variational basis, documented in
Ref.~\cite{companionNR}. No published ECSC Stark table was
available to us in full~\cite{WangChenGuo2019}; a point-by-point
comparison remains for the submission stage.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Field-free pole structure: the intermediate window}
\label{sec:fieldfree}

Reference~\cite{JiaoHo2021} leaves open what happens between
$\lambda_c=0.720524$, where the ground state ceases to be bound, and
$\lambda_{r+}=0.90035$, where sharp $s$-wave shape resonances
appear. Parametric continuation answers this directly. Tracking the
$1s$ pole across $\lambda_c$ with fine steps, the pole moves
smoothly and basis-stably into the lower half plane, but its
rotation response degrades monotonically: the two-angle spread grows
from $5\times10^{-5}$ at $\lambda=0.715$ to $10^{-2}$ at
$\lambda=1.25$, no $\theta_c$-plateau develops, and --- the
decisive diagnostic --- the tracked ratio $(\Gamma/2)/E_r$
converges to $\tan2\theta_c$ of the rotation angle used, the
signature of the rotated-continuum ray rather than of an isolated
pole. Within uniform complex scaling, then, the $1s$ pole does not
survive as an isolated resonance in the intermediate window: it
merges into the continuum, exactly as the virtual-state scenario
conjectured in Ref.~\cite{JiaoHo2021} implies. (The sharp tail
resonances above $\lambda_{r+}$ live too close to the rotated ray
for the present uniform-scaling setup; we do not attempt to
reproduce them and refer to Ref.~\cite{JiaoHo2021}.) We record the
ratio test itself as a practical artifact discriminator: any
near-threshold candidate whose width-to-position ratio tracks
$\tan2\theta_c$, or whose parameters are insensitive to $\lambda$,
is a discretized ray state, not a resonance.

The $\ell=1$ story is the clean counterpart
(Fig.~\ref{fig:2p}). The $2p$ level unbinds at
$\lambda_c(2p)=0.148205$ --- earlier than the Debye value
$0.220217$~\cite{companionNR,JiaoCritical2021}, the oscillating
tail screens harder at distance --- and detaches as a genuine shape
resonance behind the centrifugal-plus-tail barrier: near threshold
the two-angle spread is at machine level ($10^{-13}$), and the
resonance broadens rapidly as $\lambda$ grows, reaching
$\Gamma\sim\!5\times10^{-2}$ by $\lambda=0.24$.

The headline is the left panel: at every field strength the ground
resonance crosses $E_r=0$ at $\lambda^{*}\approx0.68$ --- remarkably
field-independent (from $0.686$ at $F=0.02$ to $0.681$--$0.682$ at
stronger fields, a shallow minimum near $F=0.06$; total variation
below $0.01$) --- and continues smoothly to positive energies,
reaching $E_r=+0.049$, $\Gamma=0.097$ at $F=0.02$,
$\lambda=0.9$. For $F\neq0$ the crossing is not a spectral event
--- the Stark operator's spectrum covers the real line and the
scaled operator's eigenvalues continue
analytically~\cite{HerbstSimon1978} --- but its contrast with the
field-free limit is physical and sharp: at $F=0$ the same pole
merges into the continuum upon crossing
(Sec.~\ref{sec:fieldfree}), while any finite field keeps it
isolated, because the field-ionization channel gives the pole a
finite width scale of its own and detaches it from the threshold.
The through-threshold ground resonance also has no Debye analog on
the same grid: under Yukawa screening the ground $E_r$ remains
negative over the entire map of Ref.~\cite{companionD} (its
field-free level, still bound at $\lambda=0.8$ with
$E=-0.045$, only approaches threshold at the much larger critical
screening $1.19$). Positive-energy ground resonances under extreme
Debye screening were tabulated by Yu and Ho~\cite{YuHo2005}; the
ECSC potential brings that regime down to screening parameters well
inside the physically relevant range.

Two secondary features mirror the Debye map~\cite{companionD} and
one does not. Screening again catalyzes field ionization (right
panel: at $F=0.02$ the width climbs eight orders of magnitude
across the grid), and the perturbative Stark regime again shrinks
with screening. The non-mirroring feature is the field
displacement (center panel): it turns \emph{positive} as
$\lambda\to\lambda_c$, i.e.\ near the critical screening the field
\emph{raises} the resonance position relative to the vanishing
bound level --- a threshold effect absent from the Debye map at
comparable screenings.

\subsection{Debye versus ECSC at matched binding: what the
oscillating tail does to ionization}

The comparison that isolates the tail effect must remove the
trivial difference --- at equal $\lambda$ the two potentials bind
differently. We therefore match the \emph{binding}: for each anchor
$E_b$ we solve for $\lambda_{\mathrm{Y}}$ (Yukawa) and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{E}}$ (ECSC) such that the field-free ground level
equals $E_b$ to $10^{-9}$, then compare the Stark widths at equal
$F$. Table~\ref{tab:matched} shows the result: at matched binding
the ECSC width is systematically \emph{smaller} --- by about a
factor of two at $F=0.04$ ($\Gamma_{\mathrm{E}}/\Gamma_{\mathrm{Y}}
=0.45$--$0.55$) and less at $F=0.08$ ($0.65$--$0.82$). The
quasistatic exponent is identical by construction, so the
suppression is a pure barrier-shape effect: beyond its first zero
at $r=\pi/2\lambda_{\mathrm{E}}$ the ECSC potential stops assisting
the field in lowering the Stark barrier (and briefly opposes it),
so the effective barrier is wider than its Coulomb-tailed Yukawa
counterpart. As $F$ grows the barrier peak moves inward, into the
region where the two potentials coincide, and the suppression
fades --- both trends visible in the table.

\subsection{The $n=2$ doublet}

The $m=0$ doublet at $F=0.02$ (parabolic labels $k=\pm1$ assigned
by continuation from $\lambda=0$, not quantum numbers) behaves as
under Debye screening but on a compressed $\lambda$ scale,
consistent with $\lambda_c(2p)$: the uphill component crosses
$E_r=0$ at $\lambda\approx0.094$ (Debye: $0.112$
\cite{companionD}) and continues to $E_r=+0.127$,
$\Gamma=0.079$ at $\lambda=0.30$; the downhill width roughly
triples over the same range ($0.061\to0.208$), with the ratio
$\Gamma_{-1}/\Gamma_{+1}$ drifting $1.97\to2.63$. Spreads are
below $10^{-5}$ except at the four broadest points (worst
$6\times10^{-3}$ for the downhill component at $\lambda=0.30$,
which we quote to two digits only).

\section{Conclusions}

The ECSC extension of the coupled-channel complex-scaling program
cost one identity and delivered three results: the resolution of
the intermediate-window question of Ref.~\cite{JiaoHo2021} (pole
merging, with a practical $\tan2\theta_c$ artifact discriminator as
a byproduct), the first systematic $(F,\lambda)$ resonance map
under ECSC screening --- whose ground state crosses into a
positive-energy resonance at accessible screenings, with the field
acting as the isolating agent --- and the matched-binding
comparison showing the oscillating tail suppresses field
ionization by up to a factor of two, a pure barrier-shape effect
that fades with increasing field. The machinery extends unchanged
to excited manifolds, $m\neq0$, and other screening families; the
relativistic (Dirac) version of the combined problem is the
natural next step and is under way.
